Netflix has reached a landmark agreement to acquire Warner Bros. Discovery’s studios and streaming assets, in a deal that promises to reshape the global entertainment industry. The transaction, announced last week, will hand Netflix control of Warner Bros.’ famed film and television studios as well as its premium streaming brands, while still awaiting approval from regulators in the United States and abroad.​
Deal overview
Under the terms of the agreement, Netflix will acquire Warner Bros. Discovery’s studios and streaming division in a deal valued at roughly 72 billion dollars in equity and about 82.7 billion dollars in total enterprise value. The package includes Warner Bros. Pictures, HBO and its streaming platforms, and key television production units, consolidating some of Hollywood’s best-known franchises under the Netflix umbrella.​
Strategic impact
The agreement caps a heated bidding process that saw other media giants, including Paramount, attempt to secure Warner Bros. Discovery, underscoring the strategic value of its content library and brands. With this move, Netflix positions itself not only as the dominant global streaming platform but also as one of the most powerful traditional studios, deepening its catalog with decades of films, series, and established intellectual property.​
Regulatory and political hurdles
The deal is subject to extensive antitrust scrutiny, with U.S. officials and international regulators expected to closely examine its competitive impact on the media and tech sectors. President Donald Trump has publicly signaled that the transaction could be “a problem” and indicated he expects to be involved in the approval process, adding a layer of political uncertainty to an already complex review.​
Next steps and timeline
To complete the transaction, Warner Bros. Discovery plans to first separate its global linear TV networks, including various cable and factual channels, into a standalone company, after which the studios and streaming assets would transfer to Netflix. Both sides have indicated they expect the deal to close following this spinoff, provided regulators sign off, with integration of content into Netflix projected to roll out gradually once the acquisition is finalized.​
